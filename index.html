PROJECT 03

Spacecraft orbit-keeping control — ASEN 5014

Two-person project on keeping a satellite on a 300 km circular orbit. We linearized the orbital equations of motion into a 4-state linear model, showed it is fully controllable and observable, and designed two controllers — manual pole placement and LQR — plus an observer that rebuilds the full state from radius and angle measurements. My part: the model, the controllability/observability analysis, and both controller designs. Against the 5 s settling requirement, LQR rejected disturbances about twice as fast as pole placement (1.8–2.7 s vs. 4.2 s) while staying within the thruster limits.

MATLAB · State space · Pole placement · LQR · Luenberger observerFull report available on request.
PROJECT 04

sUAS state-estimation robustness under sensor failure — ASEN 5128

Solo research paper comparing dead reckoning, a low-pass sensor-inversion estimator and a cascaded EKF in a 6-DOF nonlinear simulation of CU's Ttwistor UAS. Injected GNSS blackout, GNSS spoofing and IMU bias drift across 100-run Monte Carlo sets, and introduced a Time-to-Violation (TTV) metric: the time from sensor failure until the aircraft leaves a safe flight corridor. The EKF held the corridor for the full 50 s nominally, but under spoofing its cross-track TTV fell to 3–5 s, matching the far simpler estimator.

MATLAB · 6-DOF simulation · EKF · Monte Carlo · Fault injectionDownload paper (PDF, 1 MB)
PROJECT 05

CASTLE crewed lunar habitat — ASEN 5158 (in progress)

Mission engineer on a nine-person team designing a habitat that takes 4 crew on a 10-day mission from a 350 km Earth orbit to a 100 km lunar orbit and back, including a 3-hour spacewalk. I designed the trajectory in NASA GMAT, built the ΔV budget (8.31 km/s, or 9.14 km/s with a 10% margin, which now sets the propulsion sizing) and wrote most of the second design report, covering the mission concept, phase-by-phase timeline and top-level requirements.

NASA GMAT · Python · Trajectory design · ΔV budget · RequirementsDesign reports available on request.
PROJECT 06

Satellite orbit determination — ASEN 5044

Three-person project estimating a satellite's orbit from range, range-rate and elevation measurements taken by 12 ground stations, comparing a linearized Kalman filter with an EKF. My part: the nonlinear truth simulation, the 1,000-run Monte Carlo used to validate both filters, the NEES/NIS statistical consistency tests, and the final comparison on the provided measurement log. The tuned EKF kept position errors around one meter, inside its ±2σ bounds over the full 14,000 s run, while the linearized filter degraded as the orbit drifted from its reference.
